# The Door Colonial Could Not See

Everyone knows how the Colonial Pipeline attack began: one leaked password, a VPN account with no second factor, and 5,500 miles of fuel pipeline shut for most of a week in May 2021. That version is true, and it is the least useful one. When the company's chief executive and its lead outside investigator testified to Congress a month later, what they described was not a password problem but a door: a remote-access path the company believed was switched off, that its penetration tests never found, and that accepted a username and a password anyway. By the end of this piece you will know how to look for that door on a network you run.

## The headline

On 4 June 2021 Bloomberg ran the story that fixed the folk version: "Hackers Breached Colonial Pipeline Using Compromised Password". Its first sentence said the hack "was the result of a single compromised password". CNN carried it the same day, adding that the password belonged to "a disused virtual private networking account" without multi-factor authentication, and that it had been "part of a batch of leaked passwords found on the dark web, according to Bloomberg's interview with Carmakal". Wikipedia compresses it further: "a compromised password for an inactive virtual private network (VPN) account, which did not have multi-factor authentication enabled", in an attack that "afflicted computerized equipment managing the pipeline".

Four days after Bloomberg, the Senate Homeland Security committee opened its hearing with the same summary. "Recent news reports indicate that hackers accessed the Colonial system through a compromised password of a virtual private network (VPN) account," the chairman said. The witness was Joseph Blount, Colonial's chief executive. The next day he testified again, to the House Homeland Security committee, beside Charles Carmakal of Mandiant, the incident-response firm hired on the morning the ransom note was found. Both transcripts are published by the Government Publishing Office. Everything below about how the attacker got in comes from them.

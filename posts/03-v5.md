# The Tarball Was Not the Tree

*The xz backdoor, 2024. The story everyone tells is about a patient stranger. The record is about a file that was never in git.*

A failed login takes 0.299 seconds. Then a library update lands, and the same failed login, to a user that does not exist, takes 0.807 seconds. Andres Freund is chasing "a few odd symptoms around liblzma (part of the xz package) on Debian sid installations over the last weeks (logins with ssh taking a lot of CPU, valgrind errors)". Half a second, on a machine that is his own, and he is careful about his standing before he says anything else: "I am *not* a security researcher, nor a reverse engineer".

On 29 March 2024, at 08:51 Pacific time, he posts to the oss-security list under the subject "backdoor in upstream xz/liblzma leading to ssh server compromise". By the end of the day Red Hat has assigned CVE-2024-3094 and NVD has published it with the highest severity score it gives, 10.0, and the weakness class "embedded malicious code".

## The story everyone tells

WIRED tells it best, and its version is the one that travels. An attacker who "can spend two years politely and enthusiastically volunteering to help" a compression library that nearly every Linux distribution carries. "It's unlikely that Jia Tan is a real person, or even one person working alone." Wikipedia adds the emails of June 2022, "a pressure campaign against the original maintainer", and the suspicion of "sock puppetry". The backdoor, in this telling, was "deliberately included into the software in February 2024", and a lone engineer caught it because of a strange delay.

Hold that version. Almost all of it survives contact with the record. What the record adds is the part a defender needed, and it is not about the stranger.

## What the record can date

The commits and the list archive let us check the chronology, which is all they can check. The first patch from the address that would later sign the releases was authored on 28 January 2022 and committed by the maintainer, Lasse Collin, on 7 February.

In June 2022, on the xz-devel list, a poster named Jigar Kumar wrote that "Submitting patches here has no purpose these days. The current maintainer lost interest or doesn't care to maintain anymore". Collin answered the next day. "I haven't lost interest but my ability to care has been fairly limited mostly due to longterm mental health issues but also due to some other things". He had "worked off-list a bit with Jia Tan on XZ Utils and perhaps he will have a bigger role in the future", and "this is an unpaid hobby project". Kumar pressed again a week later. A second poster, Dennis Ens, added that "its important to be aware of your own limits". On 29 June Collin wrote that Jia Tan "has been helping a lot off-list and is practically a co-maintainer already". The emails and the handover sit in one thread. Collin does not say the emails drove it, and no document says who Kumar or Ens were.

Then git. Jia Tan's first commit as committer, not only author, is dated 30 December 2022. Every release tag from 5.4.2, in March 2023, to 5.6.1, in March 2024, names Jia Tan as tagger and carries a PGP signature block: nine tags. Among commits dated 2023 the repository records 313 authored by Jia Tan against 164 by Collin. Collin did not leave. His last commits before the 5.6.0 tag are dated two days earlier.

Collin's own commit-by-commit review, published two months after the disclosure, marks a commit of 22 January 2024 adding two RISC-V test files as "the first commit preparing for the backdoor". Three days earlier, four commits had moved the project's published URLs to a subdomain that, Collin later said, was hosted on GitHub and was the only part of the domain Jia Tan controlled. On 15 February a one-line commit added a file called build-to-host.m4 to the repository's .gitignore. Keep that name. On 23 February came "Tests: Add a few test files", five binaries including the two that carried the payload; the review calls them "Backdoor files". The 5.6.0 tag followed the next day.

Two days after that, a commit titled "Build: Fix Linux Landlock feature test in Autotools and CMake builds" placed a line consisting of a single "." inside the C snippet that CMake compiles to detect the Landlock sandbox. A bare dot is not C. The detection always failed and the sandbox was never enabled. Collin's fix, on 30 March, is titled "CMake: Fix sabotaged Landlock sandbox check. It never enabled it".

In early March the backdoor was producing crashes and Valgrind errors on some systems. Freund lists three commits of 5 and 9 March presented as fixes, then a fourth, "Tests: Update two test files", that replaced the payload. The 5.6.1 tag is dated 9 March. "Given the activity over several weeks", Freund wrote, "the committer is either directly involved or there was some quite severe compromise of their system. Unfortunately the latter looks like the less likely explanation".

So far the folk story holds. Two years and three weeks from first patch to payload; git dates the interval and says nothing about intent. A January-to-March operation rather than "February", which matters only a little. Now the part that matters a lot.

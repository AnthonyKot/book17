Excerpt: What Happened?; What Information Was Involved?; What We Are Doing?

On May 17, 2016, we became aware that data stolen from LinkedIn in 2012 was being made
available online. This was not a new security breach nor were our systems compromised.

Member email addresses, hashed passwords, and LinkedIn member IDs (an internal identifier
LinkedIn assigns to each member profile) from 2012.

We invalidated passwords of all LinkedIn accounts created prior to the 2012 breach that had
not reset their passwords since that breach. In addition, we are using automated tools to
attempt to identify and block any suspicious activity that might occur on LinkedIn accounts.

Also, since the data was stolen in 2012, LinkedIn has taken significant steps to strengthen
account security. For example, we now use salted hashes to store passwords and enable
additional account security by offering our members the option to use two-factor
authentication.

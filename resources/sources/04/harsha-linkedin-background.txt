Excerpt: section 7, Background on LinkedIn Password Breach, printed p. 12

In 2012 hackers were able to compromise LinkedIn's systems and stole over 177.5+ million
unsalted SHA-1 password hashes as well as other confidential user data.

Because the passwords were stored in unsalted form and LinkedIn was not using
key-stretching tools such as PBKDF2 or Argon2 at the time of the breach the passwords were
significantly easier to crack.

# Chapter 09 ledger — between alert and action

| Folk claim (named retelling) | Record (document, page/section) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Target's own security system produced warnings during the attack. (CBS News, “Did Target ignore data breach warnings?”) | Senate Commerce staff report, pp. 4–5 and 10, attributes to Businessweek urgent FireEye alerts and a warning sent to Target staff. | HOLDS |
| “The attack could have been prevented if Target had reacted” to those warnings. (CBS News, quoting its account of Bloomberg Businessweek) | Senate Commerce staff report, p. 10, says Target “could have potentially blocked” the malware's effect. Mulligan's prepared statement, p. 3, says different judgments “may have led to different outcomes.” Neither addresses prevention of the attack as a whole. | OVERSTATED |
| The Target breach was an HVAC-company break-in. (KrebsOnSecurity headline as a compact retelling) | Senate Commerce staff report, pp. 4–5, reports stolen contractor credentials as entry. Its pp. 4–5 and 10 also recount later FireEye warnings; Mulligan's prepared statement, p. 3, says Target then believed some unspecified intruder activity was evaluated. | MISSING |
| Target's team received the FireEye warning, had ample time, but did not act to prevent the theft. (Wikipedia, “History of Target Corporation,” citing Businessweek) | Senate Commerce staff report, pp. 1, 4–5, attributes non-reaction to Businessweek. Mulligan's statement, p. 3, concerns unspecified activity and does not resolve that account. Target's SOC alert and case records, or its completed forensic report showing receipt, disposition, rationale, and follow-up for those FireEye alerts, would settle it. | OPEN |
| Equifax's traffic-inspection certificate “had been expired for nine months.” (Wikipedia, “2017 Equifax data breach”) | House Oversight staff report, pp. 48–49 and 84, dates expiry to 31 January 2016 and intrusion onset to 13 May 2017, over fifteen months later; it calls the discovery interval nineteen months. GAO-18-559, pp. 13–15, instead relays Equifax officials' conflicting claim of about ten months before the breach. | WRONG |
| “Orgs are detecting cybercriminals faster than ever.” (The Register headline) | M-Trends 2024 infographic shows median dwell time falling from 416 days in 2011 to 10 in 2023 in Mandiant investigations. Its executive report says 54% of 2023 cases were first found externally; M-Trends 2026 reports the median rose from 11 days in 2024 to 14 in 2025, largely because of long-running case types. | OVERSTATED |

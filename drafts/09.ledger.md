# Chapter 09 ledger — evaluated, not ignored

| Folk claim (named retelling) | Record (document, page/section) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Target's own security system produced warnings during the attack. (CBS News, “Did Target ignore data breach warnings?”) | John Mulligan's prepared Senate statement, pp. 2–4, says some intruder activity was detected, logged, surfaced to the SOC, and evaluated. | HOLDS |
| “The attack could have been prevented if Target had reacted” to those warnings. (CBS News, quoting its account of Bloomberg Businessweek) | Senate Commerce staff report, p. 10, says Target “could have potentially blocked” the malware's effect; Mulligan's prepared statement says Target was assessing whether different judgments “may have led to different outcomes.” Neither establishes certain prevention. | OVERSTATED |
| “Target Hackers Broke in Via HVAC Company.” (KrebsOnSecurity headline) | Senate Commerce staff report, pp. 4–5, reports stolen credentials from an HVAC and refrigeration company as the first access, but Mulligan's prepared statement, pp. 2–4, adds the later detection, logging, surfacing, and evaluation path. Entry is only the beginning of the defensive failure. | MISSING |
| Target's security team “had ample time” but “did not act to prevent” the theft. (Wikipedia, “History of Target Corporation”) | Senate Commerce staff report, pp. 1, 4–5, says its account is based on media and expert reports and attributes the “neither reacted” claim to Businessweek. Mulligan's prepared statement says the activity was evaluated. The fetched record does not identify each recipient, judgment, or reason no effective action followed. | OPEN |
| Equifax's traffic-inspection certificate “had been expired for nine months.” (Wikipedia, “2017 Equifax data breach”) | House Oversight staff report, pp. 48–49, dates expiry to 31 January 2016 and says visibility was absent for nineteen months before discovery. GAO-18-559, pp. 13–15, uses a different interval: about ten months before the breach began. | WRONG |
| The Equifax monitoring problem was “the certificate.” (Wikipedia, “2017 Equifax data breach”) | House Oversight staff report, p. 84, records at least 324 expired SSL certificates, including 79 on devices monitoring highly business-critical domains. The singular retelling omits a certificate-management problem. | MISSING |
| “Orgs are detecting cybercriminals faster than ever.” (The Register headline) | M-Trends 2024 infographic shows median dwell time falling from 416 days in 2011 to 10 in 2023 in Mandiant investigations. Its executive report says 54% of 2023 cases were first found externally; M-Trends 2026 reports the median rose from 11 days in 2024 to 14 in 2025, largely because of long-running case types. | OVERSTATED |

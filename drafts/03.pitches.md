# Chapter 03 — pitches (pilot mode: drafting agent picked; user re-picks after reading)

## A. The tarball that was not the tree  ← CHOSEN
Anchor: Andres Freund's oss-security post (29 Mar 2024) plus the xz git history itself:
`git ls-tree v5.6.0 m4/` has no build-to-host.m4, the 5.6.0 tarball does, and Lasse
Collin's review notes date the preparation to 22 Jan 2024. Folk claim tested: "Jia Tan
inserted a backdoor into xz in February 2024" (Wikipedia lede, Wired) — true, and it hides
the one fact a defender needed, that the release artefact differed from the reviewed
source. Defense: reproducible builds as actually used in the week after (Vagrant
Cascadian's Debian rebuilds), what they verified and what they could not, and the
tarball-versus-git-archive comparison that would have. Takeaway: the thing you review
and the thing you build are not the same object unless you check.

## B. Two years of good commits
Anchor: the xz-devel thread of June 2022 (Kumar, Ens, Collin) and the per-year commit
counts from git. Folk claim: "a pressure campaign by sock puppets captured the
maintainer". Defense: OWASP's "separation of duty" bullet against a one-and-a-half
person project; the unfunded-maintainer threat model. Takeaway: the social record is
real but thinner than the retellings; the ledger would carry more OPEN rows than
verdicts.

## C. The dot in the sandbox check
Anchor: commit 328c52da (26 Feb 2024) and Collin's fix f9cf4c05 ("It never enabled
it."). Folk claim: "the backdoor" was one thing. Defense: the second sabotage shows the
category is about the pipeline, not one payload; review that reads diffs for intent.
Takeaway: a single character in a build file is a supply-chain change. Too narrow to
carry the §6 contract on its own; folded into A as a MISSING row.

## D. Why sshd
Anchor: Freund's observation that OpenSSH does not link liblzma; Debian's systemd-notify
patch does; Solar Designer's April update on OpenSSH 9.8 and systemd's dlopen change.
Folk claim: "a backdoor in SSH". Defense: the dependency edge that was removed. Takeaway:
your transitive graph is your attack surface. Strong defense record, but it does not
cover the maintainer capture that §6 requires; folded into A as a MISSING row.

## Why A
The §6 contract asks for the capture told from commits and the mailing list, the
injection explained as "what the tarball contained that the git tree did not", and
reproducible builds graded honestly. Only A covers all three from documents already in
hand. B is the most-told version and would grade mostly OPEN; C and D are the two best
MISSING rows and survive inside A. The book16 dig method (quote the commit, not the blog
about the commit) gives A its evidence without a court record, which this incident lacks.

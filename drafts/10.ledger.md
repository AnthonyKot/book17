| 6 | "CrowdStrike distributed a faulty update to its Falcon Sensor security software" (Wikipedia lede) — the update as the bug. | PIR: the update was Rapid Response Content, "not code or a kernel driver". PIR: customers could pin the sensor to N, N-1 or N-2; content was cloud-delivered and (RCA p.6) customer control over it was added *after*. A defender needed to know that the thing they could pin held the bug and the thing they could not pin pulled the trigger. | MISSING |
| 7 | "If CrowdStrike had tested the Faulty Update on even one computer before deployment, the computer would have crashed." The RCA is consistent with the allegation and never says the instance was run on a host, but it is a vendor document and the complaint is a complaint; no court has found the fact. What would settle it: a finding in *Delta v. CrowdStrike* or the independent reviews' report. | OPEN |
| 8 | "Delta estimates that it suffered over $500 million in out-of-pocket losses" (Delta complaint ¶2; ¶48 quotes the 8-K: "at least $500 million"). | A complaint.

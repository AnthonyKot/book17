# Chapter 04 — panel findings applied (2026-09-03)

Against `drafts/reviews/04-codex.md`; findings are numbered in the consolidated review's ranked order. Every changed factual claim was checked against the saved excerpt named here.

1. **Entry route (critical) — applied.** OAIC's full Background section was re-read on the official page. The chapter now says the attacker compromised a public-facing web server and used it to reach other Adobe servers, while preserving the unknown: the report does not identify the vulnerability or technique used against that server. The exact sentence is now saved in `australia-oaic-adobe-extended.txt`, the source index records the addition, and new checked claim `04-oaic-entry-route` receipts the prose.
2. **Retained data described as running old code (high) — applied.** Replaced "old code still running," "store online," and "ran beside" language with what the regulators establish: credential data remained reachable on a retained, redundant backup system. The chapter now expressly says the record does not show that the old authentication code served logins. Claim `04-three-years` was rewritten and re-checked against the OAIC dates.
3. **Defense outcome exceeded remediation evidence (high) — applied.** Removed the declaration that the reversible store no longer existed. The measurable record is now limited to decommissioning the affected server and abolishing password hints; whether another copy remained is expressly open.
4. **Replacement equated with today's OWASP prescription (high) — applied.** The chapter now says Adobe's replacement had two relevant structural properties, salt and repeated work, while the saved OAIC excerpt does not provide enough construction detail to assess it against today's named schemes. Claim `04-new-system-shape` was updated and re-checked against the OAIC and OWASP excerpts.
5. **Adobe's characterization presented as Canada's finding (high) — applied.** Both prose and ledger now attribute "sophisticated, long-term" to Adobe's description to the Canadian Commissioner. Claim `04-opc-intrusion` was re-checked against OPC paragraph 10.
6. **April 2010 attached to later parameters (medium) — applied.** The lede and defense now separate the April 2010 introduction of the replacement from Adobe's later statement about salted SHA-256 and more than 1,000 iterations. The markdown ledger makes the same distinction; claims `04-new-system-2010` and `04-new-system-shape` match it.
7. **"Every report" unsupported (medium) — applied.** The defense now limits the backup-system attribution to the Irish and Australian reports. The absence claim is explicitly scoped to the available record, and `04-hashed-store-not-taken` was rewritten and re-checked against those two excerpts.
8. **Australian counts generalized worldwide (medium) — applied.** The chapter now says that most of the Australian users counted in the report had current password data involved. It makes no inference about the worldwide distribution. Claim `04-oaic-counts` was updated and re-checked.
9. **Vendor statements used as folk stories (medium) — applied.** Removed the Adobe-announcement and LinkedIn-statement rows from both ledgers. Stajano et al.'s later "motivating story" remains as a named academic retelling, and `SOURCES.md` now identifies that ledger role. The ledger has six rows, all based on named retellings.
10. **LinkedIn ledger conclusions unsupported (medium) — applied.** The WIRED row now makes only the categorical correction that hashes are cracked or guessed, not decrypted, and says the excerpts do not establish how the reported 300,000 were recovered. The second challenged row, about 6.5 million versus 177.5 million, was removed under finding 9 instead of trying to resolve conflicting excerpts without a source.
11. **Absence of reported decryption treated as proof (medium) — applied.** Replaced "none happened" with the supported limit: the documented crossword attack did not require the encryption key, and the reports do not say it was recovered. Claim `04-crossword-record` was rewritten and re-checked against the Canadian and Irish excerpts.
12. **Users described as sharing a database row (low) — applied.** The mechanism now says users with the same password had identical ciphertext values in their respective rows.

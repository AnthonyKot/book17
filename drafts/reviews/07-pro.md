### 1. Record accuracy and secondary sourcing limit (Severity 1)
**Location:** The Mechanism section: "Carmakal said there was no evidence of phishing of the employee and no earlier attacker activity; the login used a username and a password. `<!-- CHECK: 07-cnn-folk -->`"
**The problem:** The chapter establishes a strict boundary for the record, promising that "Everything below about the intrusion comes from what those two men said under oath, and from the written statements attached to their testimony." However, the claim that there was no evidence of phishing and no earlier activity comes from a press interview (CNN quoting Bloomberg), not from the hearing transcripts. This treats a secondary source as an incident-side fact, violating the core rule that retellings are never relied on for the record.
**What would fix it:** Cut the sentence attributing the lack of phishing and earlier activity to Carmakal, as it cannot be supported by the sworn primary record. 

### 2. Ledger fairness (Severity 2)
**Location:** The Ledger row for Chairman Thompson's claim: "the FBI has since recovered most of it".
**The problem:** The verdict of OVERSTATED is unearned. The ledger row rationalizes this verdict by stating "the release records a government seizure—not recovery by Colonial." However, the folk claim explicitly attributes the recovery to the FBI ("the FBI has since recovered"), not to Colonial. A DOJ/FBI seizure of the bitcoins is exactly the FBI recovering them, meaning the primary record fully supports the claim as it was made. 
**What would fix it:** Change the verdict to HOLDS, since the DOJ press release confirms that the government successfully seized (recovered) the majority of the funds as Chairman Thompson stated.

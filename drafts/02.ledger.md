# Chapter 02 — ledger (written before the prose, 2026-09-02; rewritten to the panel's findings, 2026-09-03)

Sources: `resources/sources/02/`. Verdict vocabulary: CONTEXT §4. Each row is one folk claim from one named retelling (rows 3 and 6 pair two retellings that make the same sentence) against the document that tests it. The chapter's argument is row 2 against row 5: there were three defaults, not one; the folk story collapses them into a single "insecure until 3.6" and dates the fix to the announcement. Row 8 is the panel's correction: Horowitz's own sentence was conditional and holds; the generalisation was the reporter's (row 9).

| 1 | The attacks hit "only those left accessible via the Internet and without a password on the administrator account"; the data was "exported … and replaced" (Bleeping, 3 and 7 Jan 2017; Gevers: "This is not ransomware. Database does not get encrypted. It only gets replaced."). | Nilsson, 6 Jan 2017: "the attacker has dropped databases and/or collections and replaced them with a new one with a ransom demand". Ottenheimer, 8 Sep 2017: "users left systems insecure – connected to the Internet with no password on their Administrator account". The manual's `security.authorization`: "Default: disabled", and disabled means "A user can access any database and perform any action". Shadowserver's no-auth probe lists "the first five databases that were returned". Two conditions and a drop-and-replace; "no password" was the week's shorthand for access control off, and the vendor's two posts say what the trackers said. | HOLDS |
| 2 | MongoDB "will no longer come with an unsafe configuration out of the box" as of 3.6 (TNW, 8 Nov 2017). | SERVER-4216, filed 4 Nov 2011, closed 8 Apr 2014, fix version 2.6.0-rc0; 2.6 release notes dated 8 Apr 2014. 3.6 release notes, "Default Bind to Localhost": "From MongoDB versions 2.6 to 3.4, only the binaries from the official MongoDB RPM … and DEB … packages would bind to localhost by default." Commit 60636b4, `listen.cpp`: an empty bind meant `0.0.0.0` in the binary until April 2017. Ottenheimer, 8 Sep 2017: "since release 2.6.0 we have made localhost binding the default configuration in our most popular deployment package formats". True of the tarball and of everything built on it until 3.5.7; for the packages most people installed from, the safe bind was nearly three years old. 3.6 changed where the default lived, not whether one existed. | OVERSTATED |
| 3 | "If installed on a server with the default settings, for example, MongoDB allows anyone to browse the databases, download them, or even write over them and delete them." (Krebs, 10 Jan 2017, with a 1:55 p.m. correction "Clarified the default settings".) "Because of MongoDB's default security configuration, which allows any user full access to the database" (Wikipedia § Security). | Commit 60636b4, `listen.cpp` before the change: empty bind meant `SockAddr("0.0.0.0", port)`. SERVER-4216: the Debian `mongodb.conf` lacked `bind_ip 127.0.0.1` until 2.6.0. Matherly, Jul 2015: 2.4.14 "the last version that still listened to 0.0.0.0 by default". Manual: `security.authorization` "Default: disabled". Horowitz, Nov 2017: "if you … don't turn on authentication, we can't help you at that point." True of the tarball, the pre-2.6 packages and images built from them, and true of authentication on every version; not true of the bind on an RPM/DEB install after April 2014. One of the two default settings was never changed. | OVERSTATED |
| 4 | "About 78% of all these hosts were running known vulnerable versions"; the outdated versions "were the default MongoDB images on AWS and other cloud hosting services" (Gevers via Bleeping, 3 Jan 2017). | No published version breakdown of the January 2017 victims exists: the Gevers/Merrigan sheet's first tab has no version column, and Krebs, the Register and ZDNet quote counts, not versions. The nearest census is Matherly's of 15 Dec 2015, thirteen months earlier: most common exposed version 3.0.7 (3,010), whose packages already defaulted to localhost; "a lot of people are changing the default configuration of MongoDB to something less secure". Gevers himself, 4 Sep 2017: "Are they running on older version without safe defaults …?" A 2015 census of the exposed population shows that hosts on safe-default versions could be exposed; it is not the 2017 victim population and neither confirms nor refutes 78%. A version facet of the January 2017 Shodan results, or the sheet's victim tabs, would settle it. | OPEN |
| 5 | "MongoDB 3.6 comes hardened against database ransomware by default" (TNW headline, 8 Nov 2017); "With MongoDB 3.6, this default behavior is implemented directly in the server itself" (press release, 8 Nov 2017). The fix is dated to the 3.6 announcement, ten months after the wave. | SERVER-28229 created 7 Mar 2017, nine weeks after Nilsson's post: "All mongod and mongos 3.6 binaries shall bind to 127.0.0.1 by default", a startup warning, a `--bind_ip_all` flag. Commit 60636b4 authored 25 Apr, committed 28 Apr 2017, fix version 3.5.7. JIRA user summary, 17 May 2017. docker-library/mongo #180: "3.5.7 now binds to localhost-only by default". Ottenheimer, 8 Sep 2017: "Beginning with development release version 3.5.7". Announced 8 Nov 2017; tag `r3.6.0` 1 Dec; announce list 5 Dec: "now generally available for production deployments". Specified in March, committed in April, announced in November, shipped in December; the September wave took 26,000 more while the change sat in a development release. The gap between commit and reach is the defense story, and the folk date hides it. | MISSING |
| 6 | "Body count hits 27,000 in hours" (Register, 9 Jan 2017); "at least 29,000 … now erased" (Krebs, 10 Jan, quoting Merrigan); "more than a quarter of the 99,000 MongoDB instances open to the internet" (ZDNet, 9 Jan). | Method, from Krebs: Gevers saw Shodan listings "with names like 'readme,' 'readnow,' 'encrypted' and 'readplease'"; re-run the query and "many now have a different database title and a new ransom note". Bleeping, 7 Jan: "attackers are deleting each others' ransoms as quick as they pop up"; Gevers, 84 cases with no "trace of data exfiltration". The Gevers/Merrigan sheet: per-actor "Name of replaced DB" and "Victims (OSINT)"; totals 86,993 found, 102 helped, undated. Denominators the same week: 99,000; "more than 52,000"; ~42,000 implied by "10,500 … about 25%". A count of ransom-note names seen by a scanner, under whichever note was last written, over a denominator that varied by two. "Erased" holds; "held for ransom" mostly meant deleted. | OVERSTATED |
| 7 | "Back in 2017, Davi Ottenheimer … blamed the attacks –and rightfully so– on database owners who failed to set a password for their databases, and then left their servers exposed online without a firewall" (ZDNet, 1 Jul 2020). | Ottenheimer, 8 Sep 2017, the post ZDNet describes: "users left systems insecure – connected to the Internet with no password on their Administrator account". The same post, six paragraphs on: "since release 2.6.0 we have made localhost binding the default configuration in our most popular deployment package formats"; "Beginning with development release version 3.5.7, localhost-only binding is implemented directly in the MongoDB server"; "we added a warning to our download center". Behind it, SERVER-28229 (7 Mar 2017) and commit 60636b4 (28 Apr). The blame is quoted fairly. The other half of the same post, the vendor's own account of changing the product's default, is left out, and with it the fact that the vendor's answer to the wave was a change, not only a statement. | OVERSTATED |
| 8 | "On 3.6, localhost only is enabled by default. If you start MongoDB, you have to explicitly turn on networking. If you don't explicitly turn it on, that entire method of doing ransomware goes away" (Horowitz to TNW, 8 Nov 2017). | Commit 60636b4: an empty bind now means `SockAddr("127.0.0.1", port)`; startup warning "Remote systems will be unable to connect to this server". 3.6 release notes: "Remote clients cannot connect to the binaries bound only to localhost." Horowitz's next sentence: "If you explicitly turn it on, but don't turn on authentication, we can't help you at that point." On its stated condition. A 3.6 binary left at its default is not reachable from the internet, so the scan-connect-drop method cannot touch it; the sentence claims nothing about installs that turn networking on, and he said so in the next breath. | HOLDS |
| 9 | "Thankfully, that won't be an issue any more, as of MongoDB 3.6" (TNW, 8 Nov 2017, glossing Horowitz). | Bleeping, 4 Sep 2017: 26,000 new victims in a week, 22,449 to one group, four months after the commit; "over 45,000" cumulative. ZDNet, 1 Jul 2020: 22,900 ransomed in one campaign, "roughly 47% of all MongoDB databases accessible online"; "another 3,000 in February 2019"; causes "incorrect MongoDB configuration tutorials" and "server images that come packed with a misconfigured MongoDB system out of the box". Matherly, May 2020: the attacks "are still on-going". SERVER-28229's user summary: `bindIp: 0.0.0.0` to accept remote connections. True for a fresh install that keeps its default; not for the installed base, the images, or anyone who set the override. The waves of 2019 and 2020 fell on 3.6's watch. | OVERSTATED |
| 10 | "The default changed to localhost and the exposed count fell" (this book's CONTEXT §3 before 2 Sep 2026); "From the 60,000 MongoDB servers left exposed online in early 2017, the needle has barely moved to 48,000" (ZDNet, 1 Jul 2020). | Shodan: "nearly 30,000", 595.2 TB (Jul 2015); "at least 35,000", 684.8 TB (Dec 2015); "more than 52,000" (Jan 2017, via Krebs); exposed data "from 24 TB to 12.5 TB" 2018→2020, "the only database among the 3 … where the amount of exposed data has actually decreased", the 2018 level "already drastically lower … due to a series of ransomware attacks" (May 2020). ZDNet's 60,000→48,000 states no source; its "22,900 … roughly 47%" implies ~48,700. Shadowserver's report data was not obtained. Exposed data fell, and the wipes did part of that. Whether exposed hosts fell is not on the record; the one series that says so is unsourced. A same-method Shodan or Shadowserver host count for 2017 and 2020 would close this row. | OPEN |

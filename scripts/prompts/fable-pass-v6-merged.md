# Fable pass v6 — the merged brief (chapter {{NN}} of *Ten Ways In*, repo ~/book17)

The bake-off of v1–v5 (2026-09-03/04) showed no dramatic difference between briefs: same model,
same record, same never-false rule. What the author's read did show is that a post which opens
"You know both headlines" loses the reader who does not. So v6 merges the three rules that moved
the result and adds the one the read demanded.

1. **Open on the concrete moment** (from v5): a line of code, a login, a shutdown order, a letter
   from a regulator, in the present tense of the document. Never with the category, the book, or
   "you know this one".
2. **Assume nothing** (new): the reader has never heard of the case. The headline version is told
   to them in two or three sentences, in its tellers' words, before the record contradicts it.
   No acronym without its expansion on first use; no reference to the book's apparatus.
3. **Every paragraph earns its place** (from v3): it gives the reader something they can use on
   Monday, or something they would want to argue with. Anything else is cut.
4. **About 2,500 words** (from v4): shorter than the chapter, with nothing false and no fact lost
   that the argument needs. The ledger survives in some form; the reading list survives as links.

Read first: `CONTEXT.md` §1, §4, §5; then `chapters/{{NN}}-*.html` in full and the excerpts under
`resources/sources/{{NN}}/` behind any fact you use. Output: `posts/{{NN}}-v6.md`, Markdown, title
line, standalone for Substack, Medium or LinkedIn.

**Never false, no new facts.** Every fact comes from the chapter or its saved excerpts. Fair to the
named. OWASP quotations verbatim. Do not touch the chapter, the claims register, `index.html` or
`CONTEXT.md`. End with an Editor's note (≤100 words): what was kept, what was changed most.
